What makes a sound tactile and warm

The earlier rounds were one smooth knock that only changed pitch, so everything sounded the same and flat. A real press, like a good key or a knuckle on wood, is several things happening within 200 ms. Each theme below is a different mix of these five layers:

Where it sits in frequency. Your brief is "not too high, not flat, in between", so the themes live in the green zones and roll off the red ones:

Warm comes from three things: cutting the top end (above ~6 kHz), a touch of soft saturation that adds the 2nd harmonic (like tape or a valve), and a short, damped room instead of a long echo. Texture comes from the click being noise, not a tone, from material resonances, and from tiny random changes on every hit (±2–3% pitch, level, stereo position), so it never sounds copy-pasted.

Moments inside a theme

The theme list above is only a material test: every theme plays the same rhythm so you can compare materials fairly. That's not how the app sounds. In the app, each moment is its own gesture built from the theme's materials: a leather strap sliding, a press-stud snapping, a drawer shutting, a pencil rolling. In the app: each moment of the chosen theme is rendered once into 3–4 slightly different short audio files (about 10–30 KB each). The app picks one at random each time, so it never sounds copy-pasted, uses no CPU, and works offline through the existing sound engine. The CLI gets krnl sound test <moment>.

If we want recordings instead

Once a theme wins, it can also be recorded for real and used as files, or used as the brief to pick a pack: a thocky keyboard, a hardwood desk, a cassette deck. Options: record our own (most unique), JDSherbert Wooden UI or Stormwave Wooden Interface packs (a few dollars), or Mechvibes keyboard packs (licence differs per pack). Either way it ships inside the app and plays offline.
