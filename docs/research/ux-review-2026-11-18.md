# UX review: a week with KRNL0 (2026-11-18, v0.11.3)

The owner ran the installed 0.11.3 build like a normal user for the first time after the recent feature run. Afterwards, they asked: *does this make sense for an end user?* This review follows that week as a story. At each step it records what the user wanted, what the app did, and whether that matches. Every claim was checked in the code, and the main incident was checked in the owner's own `board.json` and time log.

Legend: **Fixed** (in this PR) · **Proposal** (needs a design draft first, per the house rules) · **OK** (works as designed; the issue is explanation, not behaviour).

---

## 0. The incident: "I ticked old tasks and they jumped to today and tomorrow"

**What the time log shows** (`timelog/2026-11.jsonl`, today, local time):

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 06:05:53.930–.937 | `plan.set` for FILMHACK Day 1, Day 2, Day 3 and premiere. All four moved to **Nov 18** inside 7 ms, each keeping its old clock time (10:00, 10:00, 10:00, 18:00) |
| 06:06:28–06:06:31 | `task.completed` for all of them, ticked one after another |

Seven milliseconds is no human drag. It is the **Welcome back** dialog, which opens after two or more days away and lists "loose ends". Its big green main button said **"Bring all 5 to today"**. The user pressed it to get out of the dialog. That moved plans that were **already done in real life but not ticked**. The user then ticked them, so they were done, but on the wrong day.

The calendar then made it worse. FILMHACK is a chain (Day 1 → Day 2 → Day 3 → premiere). With every block now at 10:00 on the same day, the chain-push rule (Decision 30, #168) stacked them end to end:

- Day 1: Wed 10:00–22:00
- Day 2: Wed 22:00 to Thu 10:00 (the ↑ at the top of Thursday is that carry-over)
- Day 3 and the premiere: Thursday

So **done** tasks were drawn on **tomorrow**.

The completion ledger also stamped all five as done at 06:06 today, so analytics count a week of work as this morning.

**Answer to the owner's question:** no, that was not correct. You did nothing wrong. Three design faults lined up:

1. **Fixed: the main button of a dialog must never be the destructive or bulk choice.** The footer now says **Keep as is** and moves nothing. The bulk move stays available as the explicit **Move all to today** button in the list header.
2. **Fixed: Welcome back assumed "not ticked" means "not done".** People forget to tick. Each row now has **Did it** (✓) and **Won't do** (⊘):
   - **Did it** ticks the task *where it was planned*, with `completedAt` set to the end of its block, so analytics count it on the right day.
   - **Won't do** uses the Todo row's existing Won't do: it takes the plan off the calendar, doesn't count it as missed and doesn't delete it.
   - CLI peer: `krnl task toggle <id> --at 2026-11-15T16:00`.
3. **Fixed: finished plans were dragged along by an unfinished one.** In `scheduleSelector`, a done anchor is now history. It keeps its own slot, is never pushed, and the chain continues after it. The amendment is recorded in Decision 30.

*Analogy:* the dialog was a "Leave" door that also emptied your backpack onto today's desk. The calendar then lined everything on the desk up in a queue, including things you had already finished.

**Your current board:** the FILMHACK tasks are already gone (deleted at 06:09–06:13). Their completions are still in the ledger, dated today. Nothing else needs fixing in the data.

## 1. Monday: planning the week

**What the user does:** writes tasks in the Todo, gives them a *When* and maybe a *Deadline*, drags some onto the calendar, and chains a few (Day 1 → Day 2).

- **OK: Plan, When and Deadline are separate**, and that is right: when you'll do it versus when it must be done. The day line (dashed deadline flags, solid "any time" chips) is clear.
- **Proposal: chains are invisible on the calendar.** Two plans in a chain look like two independent blocks. When one moves, the other moves too, and the user doesn't know why. Draw a thin connector, or a small "follows Day 1" caption on a chained block, in the calendar and in the block popup. This was the second reason the FILMHACK incident was confusing.
- **Proposal: chaining multi-day events is the wrong tool.** A chain means "do B right after A". FILMHACK Day 1/2/3 are separate fixed events on separate days. The chain added nothing, and it is what pulled them together. Options:
  - when a user links two plans whose times are on different days, ask "Keep their own days?" and keep the times, or
  - don't offer chaining for `event` plans that already have a fixed time.

## 2. During the week: doing the work

- **OK: ticking a card** shows the done card, records the completion ledger entry, and stops the pomo if it was on that task.
- **Proposal: "missed" pressure without an easy way out.** A plan whose time passed turns rust-coloured ("missed"). The one-click choices on the card are Edit time or Delete. **Won't do** exists only in the Todo row's right-click menu, so a user on the canvas reaches for Delete (this happened). Add **Won't do** to the task card's right-click menu and to the calendar block popup, next to Mark done.
- **Proposal: let a late tick say when.** Ticking a past plan always records *now*. The fix above covers Welcome back. For the card itself, a small "Done earlier?" follow-up after ticking a plan whose time has passed ("Count it on Sat 15?" with Yes/No, no extra steps when ignored) would keep analytics honest. Everything needed already exists (`task.toggle` takes `at`).

## 3. After a few days away: coming back

- **Fixed:** main button, Did it, Won't do (see §0).
- **Proposal: the dialog should open on "what happened", not "what to move".** Today the order is loose ends, then due, then habits. A calmer order: "These were planned while you were away. Which did you do?" A row of ✓ comes first, and only then "Move the rest to…". That matches what the user actually knows: what they did, not where to put it.
- **OK: "Later" vs. close.** Both close the dialog, and it doesn't come back on this launch. With the new **Keep as is** button there are now two exits doing the same thing. Consider dropping "Later" or making it really "remind me tomorrow".

## 4. Cleaning up: "I want done things off my canvas"

**The owner asked:** is there an archive? Is deleting the right way?

- **OK, but undiscoverable: done cards already leave the board by themselves** — by default overnight. Settings → *When finished cards leave the board* offers overnight, after an hour, or keep. Finished tasks stay in the Todo list under **Done**, then **Archive**, and in Analytics. Nobody finds this, because it lives in Settings and nothing on the canvas hints at it.
- **Why the cards stayed: a chain leaves only when every task in it is done.** One unticked task in a chain keeps all its done siblings on the board indefinitely. That is the likely reason the owner saw finished cards hang around and started deleting.
- **Proposal: say it where it happens.** The first time a done card stays past the cut-off because its chain is unfinished, show a one-line hint on that card: "Leaves when Day 3 is done · Archive now". Add **Archive** to the done card's right-click menu so the user decides, not the policy.
- **Answer: delete vs. analytics.** Deleting a *done* task keeps its completion: the ledger (#169) and the time log survive node deletion, and pomo time stays in its spans. Deleting an *undone* task removes it with no trace, which is fine. A skipped task should be **Won't do**, not Delete. Won't do keeps the honest record "planned, consciously skipped" and stops it counting as missed.

## 5. Canvases: "a new canvas still shows the same tasks"

**OK by design (Decision 33):** mothers and their data (Todo, tasks, Habit, Calendar, Clock, Pomo, Analytics) are shared. Only free content (text, images, frames, drawings) belongs to one canvas.

**But the word sets the wrong expectation.** "New canvas" sounds like a new, empty workspace, a fresh project. It behaves like a new **page of notes** around the same planner. Ranked options:

1. **Cheapest (copy only):** name it for what it is, for example *Pages* or *Sheets*. In the switcher, show one line under **+ New canvas**: "Your plans, habits and calendar are shared. Notes, images and drawings are per canvas."
2. **Medium:** when the user is on a canvas other than Main, offer **Focus this canvas on a tag**. The Todo and calendar then show only plans with that tag, and everything is still one data set, so analytics stay whole.
3. **Large (needs a new ADR):** separate workspaces with their own plans. This splits analytics and habits and goes against Decision 33. Not recommended before the demo.

## 6. Smaller logic and UX findings

| # | Finding | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | A done block in the future (ticked early or moved) reads as "done tomorrow". A done plan whose block is still ahead could show "done early · ✓ 06:06" instead of sitting on tomorrow as if it happened there. | Proposal |
| 6.2 | Ticking a whole chain ticks each card separately. Welcome back's **Did it** ticks the head only, and its followers reappear as their own rows. That is honest but takes extra clicks. A "Did all of it" on a chain row would help. | Proposal |
| 6.3 | The task.completed time-log event is stamped when the tick happens, even with `--at`. The ledger, which analytics use, has the real time. That is fine for the log ("when the user ticked"), but worth knowing. | OK |
| 6.4 | Settings for finished cards are stored per install (`localStorage`), not in `board.json`, so the CLI can't read or set them. Rule 8 says every function needs a `krnl` command. | Proposal (small) |
| 6.5 | The Welcome back icon-only buttons (Tomorrow, Unplan, Did it, Won't do, Show) rely on tooltips. Five unlabeled icons is the limit. A short text label on hover-expand, or grouping as [Did it] [Move ▾] [⋯], would read better. | Proposal |

## What changed in this PR

- `src/renderer/store/scheduleSelector.ts`: a done anchor keeps its own slot and is never pushed. The chain continues after it.
- `src/shared/dispatch/task.ts`: new `doneAtISO()`. `task.toggle` accepts `at` (past only) in the app and in `krnl task toggle <id> --at <time>`. The time lands in the task, its Todo row and the completion ledger.
- `src/renderer/components/Launch/WelcomeBack.tsx`:
  - each row gets **Did it** and **Won't do**
  - the main button is **Keep as is** and moves nothing
- Docs: `launch-experience.md` F14b, Decision 30 amendment, HISTORY.
- Tests:
  - selector: done successors are not pushed, and open tasks after a done one still follow the cursor
  - CLI: `--at` backdates the task, the row and the ledger, and refuses future or unreadable times
  - dialog: the main button moves nothing, Did it backdates, Won't do drops the row without deleting it

Not walked in the real window yet. The owner checks it in the app.
