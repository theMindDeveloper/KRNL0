# C — "Where did your day go?" (marketing cut)

GOAL:    a student or freelancer who plans their day in one app and tracks it in another leaves
         knowing three things: KRNL0 plans from a sentence, puts everything on one calendar, and —
         the part nothing else does — shows what you *planned against what it really took*. They tap
         "link in bio" because it is free, needs no account and keeps their plans on their computer.
         This cut sells the product's benefits; the animation only proves them.
OUTPUT:  HyperFrames, 1080 x 1920, 30 fps, ~45 s. Narrated (ElevenLabs Eleven v4, tags), word captions,
         quiet effects. The voice is the clock.

THE ONE THING:  "Your tasks take longer than you plan, and KRNL0 shows you by how much."
THE SPINE:      one real plan, "Review the proposal 11:00 1h", typed in the first scene, landing on the
                calendar, tracked by the timer, and found at the top of "planned and spent":
                planned 1h, took 2h05. (Numbers are demo data from the film's own board.)

PROOF, NOT DECORATION — every claim on screen is the real app doing it, and uses the website's own words:
  hook   "Where did your day go?"                       → real Clock, 20 minutes of the evening slipping by
  say    "Type it. It is planned."                      → the real quick-add card: Today · 11:00 · 1 h
  see    "One calendar for all of it."                  → the typed plan lands as the 11:00 block
  run    "Press start. It records what you do."         → pick the plan in the Timer, press Start, it counts
  truth  "See where the time went."                     → Analytics → "Planned vs spent": Review the proposal 2h05 / 1h
  trust  "Free. No account. Your plans stay on your computer."   (the site's own line)
  CTA    "Free download · link in bio", Windows · macOS · Linux.

LOOK:    the website's day colours, flat: acid #c9f158 (hook, focus, end), lavender #a98bf6 (plan),
         cyan #22d3ee (calendar), paper #f4efe2 (review, "see where the time went"), dark #141414 (trust).
         Geist 800 at 130 px / -5.5% for headlines; JetBrains Mono 700 38 px for the time chip;
         captions Geist 600 46 px. Ghost clock digits tone-on-tone behind each scene, as on the site.
         Windows whole, 860 px wide (calendar 700), one shadow, rounded to the app's 8 px radius.

MOTION:  expo.out arrivals, power3.in exits, scene change = the next ground wipes up from the bottom
         (0.55 s). Headlines blur-in word by word then hold dead still. One hero move per scene; no
         cube tricks, no particles.

BANNED:  animation for its own sake; a window cropped or smaller than 860 px (calendar 700); any claim
         the product does not make; real brand logos; a dark void as the main ground (only the trust
         beat, with big type); the same whoosh on every cut; effects louder than the voice.

QA:      each beat: the headline reads in <1 s, the window is whole, the action is visible, the voice
         line lands on the action. Phone sheet at 360 px. Voice and effects measured.
